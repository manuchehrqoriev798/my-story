## Make the Choice Right

### Chapter Two

> Do not choose the right choice. Make the choice right.
> — M.Q.

Most of the anguish around a decision is spent before it, not after. People
build a model in which one of the doors is correct and the others are
mistakes, and then they stand in the hallway, sometimes for years, trying to
identify the correct door from the outside.

The model is wrong. There is very rarely a correct door. There is a door you
walk through and then spend the next several years making correct, and there
are doors you did not walk through, which stay perfect forever precisely
because you never tested them.

This is not an argument for carelessness. It is an argument about where the
work is. The work is not in the choosing. The work is on the other side.

> When you are young, I think it is very important to risk more. Try many
> different things, and try to learn as many things as possible.
>
> If you put your goals high, even though you do not reach that goal, you
> will end up very close to it. So aim higher.
> — D.A.

Aiming higher is not motivational decoration. It is arithmetic about where
you land when you miss, and you will miss.

And once the door is chosen, the discipline reverses completely.

> Put the goal and go towards it. No left, no right, until you achieve your
> goal. Do not slack off.
> — L.A.

Two different modes, and using the wrong one at the wrong time is how people
waste a decade. Before the decision: try everything, risk more, look in
every direction. After it: no left, no right. The people who suffer most are
the ones who are cautious before choosing and distractible after.

Two more, from nobody in particular and everybody in general, that belong
here:

> Ask for forgiveness, not permission.
>
> Ideas are worthless.

The second one is not cynicism. It is a statement about which side of the
door value lives on.
