## There Are No Failures

### Chapter Seven

> There are no failures. There are learning opportunities.
> — I.C.

This one is close to a cliché, and it survives being a cliché because of what
it actually asks you to do, which is difficult and specific: extract the
lesson *while the loss is still fresh*, not years later when it has been
smoothed into an anecdote.

The extraction has a deadline. A failure you did not examine within a
reasonable window does not sit there patiently waiting to teach you. It
converts into a story about yourself, and stories about yourself are very
hard to revise.

> Sweet is only sweet because you have bitter to compare it with.
> — I.C.

Not consolation. Structure. The scale requires both ends to exist or it does
not measure anything. A life engineered to remove every bitter reading would
not become uniformly sweet, it would become flat, and flat is not the good
outcome people imagine when they are avoiding difficulty.

> Whatever dark moments you face in life, responding with bitterness is not
> the answer. Choose to reflect them with goodness instead.
> — Sh.

This is the hardest instruction in the book and I want to be honest about
why, rather than presenting it as easy wisdom.

Bitterness is *earned*. That is what makes it so difficult to put down. When
someone has actually wronged you, bitterness feels like the accurate
response, the honest one, the one that respects what happened. Choosing
goodness instead can feel like lying about the severity.

It is not. It is a decision about direction, not about the facts. The facts
stay exactly as bad as they were. What changes is whether the damage
terminates with you or continues through you into everyone downstream.

> Even when faced with negativity, choose to absorb, learn, and shine with
> kindness.
> — M.Q.

Absorb, learn, shine. In that order, and the middle step is the one people
drop. Absorbing without learning is just taking damage. Shining without
absorbing is performance.
