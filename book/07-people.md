## The People In the Room

### Chapter Five

> Be careful with the people around you, and have a trustworthy group of
> people.
> — L.A.

You do not have a personality so much as an average, and the things being
averaged are mostly sitting near you. This is not a metaphor about influence.
It is closer to a mechanical fact. Standards, vocabulary, what counts as
normal effort, what counts as an acceptable excuse: all of it is set locally,
by the five or six people you actually talk to, and almost none of it is set
by you deciding.

Which makes choosing those people one of the highest leverage decisions
available, and one almost nobody makes deliberately.

> Have a mentor, or a person who can see your position and your decision from
> outside, and give you feedback.
> — A.KH.

The value is not the advice. The value is the vantage point. You cannot see
your own position from inside your own position, and no amount of
intelligence fixes that. It is a geometry problem, not an ability problem.

> Being in a relationship is like an investment. You can grow, stay neutral,
> or fall. But no matter the outcome, a lesson should always be learned.
> — M.Q.

There is a hard limit on all of this, and it is worth stating plainly
because a lot of good energy dies against it:

> If a person does not want to help himself, no one else can.
> — M.Q.

Not *will not*. Cannot. This is not permission to stop caring about people.
It is the boundary of what caring can accomplish, and knowing where that
boundary sits is what lets you keep caring for decades instead of burning
out in two years and deciding people are not worth it.

And then the debt, which is the part most people skip:

> Be grateful to everyone who supported you: those who guided, helped, or
> simply shared their time. Thank you for being part of my journey. Because
> someone did good for me, I will strive to do good for others. Even when
> faced with negativity, choose to absorb, learn, and shine with kindness.
> — M.Q.

This book exists because of the initials at the bottom of these quotes. None
of them were writing a book. They were talking.
