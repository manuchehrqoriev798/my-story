## Diet From What Does Not Match

### Chapter Six

> Diet and work hard like a sportsman. Diet from stuff and people that do not
> match your goals.
> — M.A.

The word diet is doing something unusual here. A diet is not primarily about
adding. It is a rule about subtraction, applied continuously, to things you
would otherwise consume by default. Extending it past food is the whole
insight: the same operation works on commitments, on inputs, on people, on
open tabs.

Most people try to reach their goals by addition only. More effort, more
projects, more hours, more contacts. Addition has a ceiling and it arrives
fast, because the day does not get longer. Subtraction has no ceiling.

> Not everything you love eating or doing is good, healthy or beneficial. I
> know you know this, but sometimes we all forget it.
> — L.A.

The parenthetical is the important part. *I know you know.* Almost nothing
in this chapter is new information to anyone. The failure is never a failure
of knowledge. It is a failure of the gap between knowing and the specific
Tuesday evening where the knowing is not present in the room.

> Working is great, but do not forget to rest too.
> — E.

Rest is not the opposite of the discipline in this chapter. It is inside it.
An athlete who does not sleep is not more disciplined than one who does,
they are worse at the same job. Treating recovery as the thing you cut first
is the most common way that hard working people quietly become unproductive
while feeling maximally virtuous about it.

> Do not run after beer, women and idiots when you are young.
> — D.

Blunt, and the bluntness is the point. It is the same subtraction rule with
the abstraction removed, said by someone who was not trying to be quotable.
