## Unfair to Everyone

### Chapter One

> Life is fair because it is unfair to everyone.
> — M.Q.

People say life is not fair as though that were the end of the sentence. It
is not the end of the sentence. It is the beginning of one. Life is not
unfair to you specifically. It is not running a campaign against you. It is
unfair to the person you envy, in a way you cannot see from where you are
standing, and it was unfair to the person they envy, and so on up a ladder
that has no top.

Once you actually believe that, the question changes. It stops being *why
me* and becomes *what do I do with this particular unfairness*. The first
question has no answer. The second one always does.

> You often compare yourself to people who seem ahead of you, forgetting that
> what you already have is someone else's dream. That does not mean you
> should not work harder. The people ahead of you did not do anything
> impossible to get there. They are just like you. They simply put in a bit
> more effort and stayed consistent.
> — R.

There is a trap on the other side of this, and it is the more dangerous one,
because it disguises itself as maturity.

> Sometimes you think you are being honest, fair, realistic. But when you
> reflect on your own situation and compare it with others, you realise you
> are actually being pessimistic. You are far more capable than you think,
> and you truly deserve it. Try to be realistic, but do not become overly
> pessimistic by doubting yourself.
> — M.Q.

Pessimism is the cheapest way to sound serious. It costs nothing, it is
never embarrassed, and it is wrong roughly as often as optimism is. The
difference is that optimism occasionally gets you into the room.

Realism is not the midpoint between hope and despair. Realism is looking at
what is actually true, including the parts that are true in your favour, and
most people auditing themselves quietly skip that column.
