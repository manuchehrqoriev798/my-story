## Slowly, But To the Goal

### Chapter Four

> Do not be in a hurry. Go slowly, but to the goal.
>
> Keep working, but be calm. Let your result speak.
> — I.C.

Speed and direction are independent. This is obvious in physics and almost
nobody applies it to their own life. Enormous energy gets spent going
quickly in a direction that was never checked, and the people doing it feel
productive the entire time, because effort feels like progress from the
inside.

Going slowly to the goal beats going quickly to nowhere, and it is not
close.

> Good things take time.

> Stress does not come from hard work. Stress primarily comes from not taking
> action over something you can have some control over.
> — V/B.

Read that second one twice. It reassigns blame for a feeling almost everyone
misattributes. Exhaustion comes from work. Stress comes from the gap
between what you could act on and what you have acted on. Which means the
cure for stress is almost never rest. It is usually one specific action you
have been avoiding, and you already know which one.

> Do not worry, and do your best. By best I mean really best. Leave the rest
> to opportunity.
> — A.KH.

The split matters. Your best is fully yours. Opportunity is fully not yours.
Anxiety is what happens when you try to take responsibility for the second
category, and it does not work, and you keep trying.

> Let your result speak.
> — I.C.

There is a version of ambition that is loud and a version that is quiet, and
they look identical for the first two years. After that they stop looking
identical.

> What counts, I found, is not what you cover, but what you uncover.
> — W.L.

Coverage is how much ground you crossed. Uncovering is how much of it you
actually saw. A course finished, a book read to the last page, a repository
scrolled through: all coverage, all worth close to nothing on their own. The
one idea you found and cannot now stop seeing everywhere is the entire
return.
