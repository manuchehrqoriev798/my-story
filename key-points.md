## One Outfit in Every Photo

### 2018, eighth grade

A bootcamp in Khujand. I took a lot of photos there. Came back, showed them,
and the one thing people pulled out of a whole week was the clothes.

> Why do you only have one outfit?

Not how was it. Not what did you learn there. The wardrobe.

**What it taught.** You go somewhere to rest. You do not go in order to show
that you are resting, and you do not pack for the photographs.

## The Birthday Nobody Could Count

### 2019, ninth grade

Somebody I knew spent his birthday counting. Not the people who said
something to him, the places where it could be seen that they had.

⟨The scene itself stays in the workshop, because the person in it never
agreed to be in a book. What comes out here is the mechanism, and the
mechanism does not need him.⟩

**What it taught.** One day a year is countable, so it is the day that gets
counted. The support underneath it, running through the other three hundred
and sixty four, is not countable, so it does not get counted at all. ⟨Which
is the same word three separate people gave you: unconditional.⟩

## The Cobbler With No Boots

### 2025

*Сапожник без сапог.* The cobbler who makes boots for the whole street and
walks barefoot himself.

I give advice. I know the thing I am telling other people, and I am right
about it. My own acting on it is low.

⟨When in 2025 you saw it. What advice you had just given, and what you were
not doing yourself while you gave it.⟩

Knowing something and saying it out loud are the same cheap act, and neither
one of them is doing it. The distance between the two is not a knowledge
problem, which is why more reading never closes it.

**What it taught.** More acting. ⟨Your words. This is the one chapter where
the lesson is an instruction to yourself rather than something you noticed
about the world, and it should sound like one.⟩
